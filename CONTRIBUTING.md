# Contributing to Martialis

Suggestions are very welcome: additions, corrections, sources, links and discussion. Send them
through Discussions. Use Issues for the model and tools, and for requests about your own personal
data (see [Living persons](#living-persons)).

Pull requests are not used, so do not open one.


## Scope of the database and inclusion criteria

The goal of Martialis is ambitious: to create the most comprehensive, transparent, sourced database
of martial knowledge transmission, and because of that there's an important question that should be
answered: what is in scope for the database, and what isn't?

Martialis aims at building, first and foremost, the core tree of martial knowledge transmission: who
taught whom, where, and in which style. To achieve this, we must focus on the main trunk and
branches, and not every individual practitioner: the author of this project, for example, has no
entry in the database.

This is not a judgement of anyone's rank or skill: every entry must rest on evidence that someone
other than the person can check, and thousands of entries with a single self-reported link each
would reduce the usefulness of the database and derived tree.

### What qualifies

A person, style or organisation can be added when at least one of these is true:

* **Independent coverage.** An encyclopedia with its own inclusion policy, such as Wikipedia
  in any language, has an article on them.
* **A published source names them in a meaningful role**: a book, an article, or the official
  record of a style or organisation names them as a teacher, student, founder, or holder of a
  title or licence. A passing mention, or a source written by the person themselves, is not
  enough on its own.
* **They are a link in the tree.** They taught people who are themselves in the database, or
  they connect an existing entry to its teacher, and a source shows it.

Note that teaching classes, running a club or holding a grade does not qualify by itself: most
committed practitioners do these things. Suggestions about yourself are welcome as leads, but they
need one of the points above from someone else. Being the author of a cited source does not create
an entry either: an author has an entry only when they meet the points above in their own right.


### If you don't qualify (yet)

If none of the points above applies to you, you can still connect to Martialis:

* **Link yourself on [martial.is](https://martial.is).** A profile can name your teachers from the
  database and be shared. This is the intended place for personal lineage, and it needs no source.
* **Help improve the line above you.** Your teacher, or their teacher, may be missing and may
  qualify: filling in the line above you is one of the most useful contributions there is.

You can always suggest entries, but consider the above guidelines that help maintain the focus and
quality of the database.

## Living persons

Martialis records what public and published sources say, and the evidence is open to debate in
Discussions. Disagreeing with an entry therefore usually means disagreeing with a source. A source
can be wrong, but that leads to a conflicting source being added next to it, not to removal:
recording sources that disagree is part of the model. Requests to change or remove what a cited
source says are not accepted on that ground alone.

People recorded in the database can also ask for changes that improve the entry or reflect their
preferences, such as a corrected date of birth, a better photograph, or the preferred form
of their name. These are discretionary and handled case by case.

Both are separate from your rights over personal data (rectification, objection, erasure), which
are described in the [privacy policy](https://martial.is/privacy#database). Open an Issue for such a
request: it keeps the request and its answer public, like the database. If you prefer, email the
address given in the privacy policy instead.


## How to contribute

**Create or add to a Discussion**: follow the instructions there, but the short version is that
discussion topics should have the ULID of the entity in the title, since that's how they get picked
up by the web application. All discussions are public, so consider that when contributing.

## What a good suggestion looks like

- **Every claim cites at least one source**, with a locator and a direct quotation where the wording
  matters.
- **Disagreeing sources are included**, not left out.

Suggestions can be made in any language that I can translate: this means several European languages
directly, but more importantly it means that it is not required that comments are made in English:
when discussing someone from Germany, it's perfectly acceptable to contribute in German, for
example. I will do my best to translate, which given today's technology is simple.

## Licensing

By submitting content for the database, in a Discussion, an issue, or the suggestion form on
martial.is, you dedicate it to the public domain under **CC0-1.0**, covering copyright and any sui generis database right you may hold, and you
represent that it is your own statement of fact with citations rather than material copied from a
copyrighted or database-rights-protected work. Short quotations given as evidence are not part of
that dedication: they stay with their rightsholders. The database itself stays CC-BY-SA-4.0. See
[LICENSING.md](LICENSING.md#inbound-licensing).

For a substantial donation (a whole researched lineage, scans, photographs), contact the maintainer
to arrange a short individual agreement.

Disagreements are settled through the debate in Discussions; when consensus fails, the project lead
decides and states the reasoning.
